What a day actually looks like

Core skills in the morning. A quest in the afternoon. Their own mess cleaned up before they go.

Arrival to 8:30

Free play

Children arrive and play. Nothing is scheduled. It sounds small, and it is one of the reasons they can concentrate so hard for the rest of the morning.

8:30

Socratic launch

The studio circles up and a guide poses a genuinely difficult question. During the pandemic one of them was this: if you lead a country, is it more important to protect people's freedom while risking their health, or to protect their health while risking their freedom? There is no right answer. That is the point. Leaders live there, and we are preparing them for it.

Morning

Core skills

Several hours of deep, self directed work on the goals each learner set for themselves on Monday. Maths and language are mastered at 100 percent before anyone advances a level.

Midday

Lunch, served family style

Real homemade food on real plates, with real glasses and real silverware. No disposables, no processed food, no added sugar. When a child drops a ceramic plate it breaks, and they learn something a plastic cup could never teach them.

Afternoon

The quest

A four to six week sprint of escalating challenges: biology, physics, civilisation, personal development, or building a business from nothing. Each quest ends in a public exhibition where families and working professionals come to see the results. In the Children's Business Fair they build a business over six weeks and then sell to real customers with real money on the line.

Before they leave

Studio maintenance

Learners clean their own desks, their own room, the floors and the cafeteria. We do not employ someone to do it for them. Independence is not a poster on the wall, it is a series of daily decisions.

Art, gym, history and music run through the week as well, the same as anywhere else.

The Next Great Adventure

Our graduates do not go to university to figure out what they want. They arrive already knowing.

Most people finish an expensive education and still cannot answer the only question that matters. We built a three phase programme into high school so that no learner leaves here without an answer they tested themselves.

Phase one

Searching

Learners explore what genuinely pulls them and then go and find out. They call working professionals themselves and run informational interviews. One of our learners interviewed two former Salvadoran secretaries of state, then decided politics was not for her. Discarding a path with evidence is a win.

Phase two

Exploring

They find their own apprenticeships. Not the parents, not the school. Forty hours in the first year, eighty in the second, one hundred and twenty in the third. In the third year the work has to be paid, because if nobody will pay you, how valuable were you really?

Phase three

Committing

In their final year they stand in front of their family and their own personal board of directors and give a speech: this is what I am doing next, this is why, and this is what I need from you. In the wider Acton network, funding for a first business has come out of that speech.

Our first graduate, June 2026. He is now in medical school in El Salvador.

Our first graduate apprenticed with an orthopaedist, a neurologist, a neonatologist and a dermatologist. He watched real surgeries. Friends outside Acton talked him out of medicine because the degree was too long. Then, on his first day at a distribution company, a woman collapsed in front of him and nobody knew what to do. He said he never wanted to be in that position again. He recommitted to medicine and he is in medical school now. He is eighteen. That kind of conviction is not an accident, and it is not common.

A school in El Salvador is never only a school. It is the community your family joins.

Honestly, who is this for?

We are not for everyone, and we would rather say so on the website than on the phone.

Families who thrive here

  • Entrepreneurs, builders and independent thinkers who want the same for their children
  • Parents who believe children grow strong through responsibility and real challenge
  • Families who prefer natural consequences to adult micromanagement
  • People who value grit, curiosity and a growth mindset over status and belonging
  • Families willing to risk being outside the crowd in order to do something better
  • Parents who care more about who their child becomes than which group they belong to
  • Children with high agency who want real challenges and real consequences
  • Children who feel bored and trapped being passive

Families who should choose elsewhere

  • Those who want highly structured, adult led instruction with standard outcomes
  • Those who need daily updates, constant communication or heavy teacher intervention
  • Those who expect traditional grading, pacing and a top down curriculum
  • Those who want adults to solve their child's conflicts and supply their motivation
  • Those uncomfortable with autonomy, risk taking or natural consequences
  • Those looking for loopholes, exceptions or special treatment
  • Those who want the big soccer team, the gymnasium and the classic high school experience
  • Children who need one to one instruction or specialised support, which we are not equipped to provide

We do not offer specialised services for children with special needs. Acton is a fast moving, highly independent environment, and we would rather be honest than let a child struggle in the wrong setting.

Questions parents actually ask

The five that come up on almost every call.

How much does Acton Academy El Salvador cost per month?

Acton Academy K-12 costs $683 per month, everything included. That is $440 tuition, $118 for the healthy food program, $80 for materials, books and subscriptions, and $45 for the annual enrollment fee spread across the year. Montessori Preschool is $382 a month half day and $557 a month full day on the same basis.

There is also a one time registration fee of $1,800 for children five and older, which Salvadoran citizens and permanent residents pay at the start of their second year. There are no uniform fees, book lists, technology fees, library fees or supply lists on top of the monthly figure.

Is Acton Academy El Salvador accredited?

Yes. We are accredited in the United States by IALDS and we work with two umbrella organisations, West River Academy and The Forest School. We issue official US transcripts with an apostille, which families take to MINED to complete the homologación.

That gives graduates the official documentation to enter higher education in El Salvador and in other countries. Our first graduate completed exactly that process in June 2026 and is now studying at a Salvadoran university.

Are you accredited by MINED in El Salvador?

No, and that is on purpose. Accepting MINED accreditation would require us to change the way we teach, which would lower the quality of what we offer. Every public school in the country holds that accreditation, so it does not by itself tell you anything about the education.

What parents are really asking is whether their child will have the piece of paper to go to university here. The answer is yes, through the US transcript and the homologación, and we have now proved it with a real graduate.

What is the difference between Montessori and Acton?

Acton keeps every principle of Montessori: independence and autonomy, self direction at your own pace, concrete hands on learning, practical life skills like cooking and cleaning up after yourself, and grace and courtesy.

Acton then adds three things Maria Montessori never had. Socratic discussion, modern technology, and entrepreneurship. Our preschool is authentic Montessori, led by certified Montessori guides. From kindergarten upward it is the Acton model built on those same Montessori foundations.

How do you prevent bullying?

We are extremely careful about who comes in. Every applicant aged six and up spends a full trial week with us. At the end of it, the children in that studio vote on whether to invite them in, judged against the qualities of an Acton hero: intentionality, civility, energy and excellence. The vote has to be unanimous.

If it is a yes, the children write the family a letter themselves. If it is borderline, they give specific feedback and invite the child back for a second week. We also hold families to an honour code, and we have exited families where a pattern of lying, cheating or aggression continued. It is hard to get in, and that is exactly why it is a kind place to be.
